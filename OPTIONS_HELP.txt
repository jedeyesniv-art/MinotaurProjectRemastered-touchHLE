View options:
    --fullscreen
        Display the app in full screen. The default is to display in a window
        instead, except on Android which always uses full screen.

        The app's output will be scaled to fit your screen. This is independent
        of the internal resolution, so if the scale hack (see below) is not also
        in use, the result may be a bit blurry. An internal resolution larger
        than your screen's is possible, in which case the output is downscaled.

    --upside-down
    --landscape-left
    --landscape-right
        Changes the orientation the virtual device will have at startup.
        The default is portrait.

        --upside-down means rotate 180° from portrait.
        --landscape-left means rotate 90° counterclockwise from portrait.
        --landscape-right means rotate 90° clockwise from portrait.

        Usually apps that require landscape mode will tell touchHLE about this,
        and it will automatically rotate the window, but some apps neglect to
        do this. These options may be useful in that case.

    --dpi-aware
        (Private fork, Windows.) Tell Windows that touchHLE handles high-DPI
        displays itself, so the window is not bitmap-stretched (which blurs
        it) when display scaling is above 100%. Window sizes become physical
        pixels, so combine with --window-scale= to keep a useful size.

    --window-scale=...
        (Private fork.) Make the window this many times the app's screen size,
        e.g. --window-scale=2. Unlike --scale-hack, the app still renders at
        its normal resolution. Use with --nearest-present for crisp pixels.

    --integer-scaling
        (Private fork.) In full screen, scale the app's output by the largest
        whole number that fits, centred with black borders.

    --nearest-present
        (Private fork.) Scale the app's output to the window with
        nearest-neighbour sampling instead of bilinear filtering. Sharpest
        with whole-number scale factors.

    --nearest-textures
        (Private fork.) Force nearest-neighbour filtering for all of the app's
        textures. Can make pixel art sharper, but may make effects that rely
        on smooth filtering (glows, blurs) look blocky.

    --perf-log=FILE
        (Private fork.) Once a second, write a line to FILE (a CSV file,
        relative to the touchHLE folder) with the frame rate, how much drawing
        and uploading the app asked for per frame, and how long the GPU spent
        on the app's drawing and on touchHLE's presenting. Press F9 to mark a
        moment, e.g. --perf-log=touchHLE_perf.csv.

    --hide-cursor=SECONDS
        (Private fork.) Hide the mouse pointer over the window once the mouse
        has been still for this long (default 2; 0 = never). It comes back as
        soon as the mouse moves. F11 or Alt+Enter switches between fullscreen
        and a window.

    --ambient-bars
    --ambient-bars=BRIGHTNESS,SECONDS,NEAR,SATURATION
        (Private fork.) Fill the bars beside (or above and below) the picture
        with a soft glow of its own colours, each bar taking the colours from
        its side of the picture, like an "ambient light" TV. Right beside the
        picture it is bright (NEAR, default 1.5) and saturated (SATURATION,
        default 1.8; 1 = as is), fading to BRIGHTNESS (default 0.45) at the
        screen's edges. SECONDS (default 0.4) smooths it so it drifts between
        colours rather than flickering. Leave values off the end for their
        defaults, e.g. --ambient-bars=0.45,0.4,2. Not drawn while a --bezel is
        showing.

    --deflex-pad
        (Private fork, Deflex.) Controller support that follows what's on
        screen: A starts from the title; on the level select the left stick
        moves a pointer, A drags with it and X presses PLAY; in play X, LB, LT,
        L3, d-pad left or stick left hold the left paddle button and B, RB,
        RT, R3, d-pad right or stick right the right one, and Start pauses;
        when paused Start resumes, X retries and B quits; after a level X
        retries, B quits and A goes to the next; B (or Esc) on the title or
        level select quits touchHLE.

    --edge-bars=POINTS
        (Private fork.) Fill the bars beside an upright portrait picture with
        one colour: the average of the picture's outermost POINTS on both
        sides, their whole height (sampled ten times a second, easing over
        0.4 s), e.g. a game's cavern walls. Takes the place of
        --ambient-bars, except where a game's pad support switches to the
        glow (Caverns of Minos' menus).

    --edge-bars-texture=FILE,POINTS
        (Private fork.) With --edge-bars, tile this picture (next to
        touchHLE) over the bars, each of its pixels POINTS screen points
        across, multiplied by the bars' colour: white shows the colour itself,
        grey darker flecks, the way games tint a grey-and-white texture. The
        colour is first divided by the picture's average, so the bars' base
        colour matches the original rather than the averaged one.

    --fake-classes=PREFIX,...
        (Private fork.) Also replace Objective-C classes whose names start
        with these prefixes with fakes that ignore every message, as touchHLE
        already does for OpenFeint*, FB* and ad SDKs. E.g. --fake-classes=OF
        turns off all of OpenFeint, whose services otherwise crash with
        OpenFeint itself faked.

    --minos-pad
        (Private fork, Caverns of Minos.) Controller support that follows
        what's on screen, alongside --stick-to-icade. On the title, menu,
        info pages and pause the left stick moves a pointer and A touches;
        Start is the iCade button on the title and menu (select) and MORE on
        the info pages. In play the left stick is the iCade joystick and A
        the iCade button (thrust). Back (or Esc) quits from the title or
        menu, presses DONE on the info pages (B too) and QUIT when paused;
        Start pauses and unpauses; on the menu X presses INFO and d-pad
        left/right the difficulty - and +. In play the right trigger
        thrusts too: fully pulled it holds thrust, and part pulled it pulses
        it, longer pulses the further it's pulled.

    --audio-resampler=NAME
        (Private fork.) How an Audio Unit's sound (a game's own mixer, e.g.
        Super Ox Wars at 11025 Hz) is converted to the output rate: point,
        linear (OpenAL Soft's default), cubic, fast_bsinc12, bsinc12,
        fast_bsinc24 or bsinc24. Low-rate audio sounds gritty with linear;
        bsinc24 filters it cleanly, as the iPad's own converter does.

    --volume=FACTOR
        (Private fork.) Scales an Audio Unit's sound (a game's own mixer) by
        FACTOR, from 0 to 1: e.g. --volume=0.5 halves it (Goat Up 2's mix is
        very loud).

    --starfield-bars
        (Private fork, Goat Up.) Fills the bars beside the picture with
        falling stars on black, like the game's own background: soft dim dots
        in bright colours, each falling straight down at its own speed. It
        takes the place of --ambient-bars.

    --trim-edges=PIXELS
        (Private fork, Goat Up 2.) When there are bars beside the picture
        (fullscreen), blackens PIXELS screen pixels at the picture's left and
        right edges, hiding a line of garbage some games leave there.

    --starfield-bars=warp
        (Private fork, Goat Up 2.) The same, but white stars flying out from
        the picture's centre in 3D, growing and brightening as they come
        closer, like the game's own background: the bars carry on its flight.

    --goat-pad
        (Private fork, Goat Up.) Controller support that follows what's on
        screen, alongside --left-stick-tilt (the game's control type C): A
        jumps in play (held for a long jump) and on the menu presses TOUCH TO
        START; X changes the control type and Y presses MORE INFO; B (or Esc)
        quits from the menu, presses MAIN MENU on the info pages and QUIT when
        paused; Start pauses and unpauses; on the info pages A presses MORE
        INFO.

    --goat2-pad
        (Private fork, Goat Up 2.) Controller support that follows what's on
        screen, alongside --left-stick-tilt (the tilt control type): in play
        A jumps (held for a long jump); on the title A begins and B (or Esc)
        quits; on the menus the left stick moves a pointer and A touches with
        it, B quits from the game select and goes BACK from the level select;
        Start pauses and unpauses; when paused Y is RESET, B is Exit and X
        changes the control type.

    --rescue-pad
        (Private fork, Minotaur Rescue iOS.) Controller support that follows
        what's on screen: in play the left stick strokes a virtual finger
        (like the PC beta's TOUCHY stick), Start double-taps the sun to
        pause and A taps the middle (game over). Everywhere else the left
        stick moves a pointer and A touches (held, it drags, which scrolls
        the text pages). Title: Start begins, Back (or Esc) quits; main
        menu Start PLAY, X INFO, Y the game select banner, Back quits; info
        Start/X/Y its three pages, B or Back BACK; text pages B or Back
        BACK; level select d-pad the level, X SURVIVAL, Start START, B or
        Back BACK; pause Start UNPAUSE, Back QUIT GAME, d-pad the audio
        level.

    --rescue-turn-fix
        (Private fork, Minotaur Rescue iOS 1.0.) Fixes the game's ship turn,
        which went the long way round (a spin or twitch) whenever the turn
        crossed pointing left, as a left stroke usually does.

    --rescue-touch-speed=SPEED
        (Private fork.) --rescue-pad's finger speed at full tilt, in screen
        heights per second. Default 1.0.

    --left-stick-tilt[=DEGREES]
        (Private fork.) The same as --right-stick-tilt, on the left stick.

    --right-stick-tilt[=DEGREES]
        (Private fork.) The device stands upright in portrait and the right
        stick's left/right turns it like a steering wheel, up to DEGREES each
        way (default 40), for the accelerometer and Core Motion alike.
        Holding the right mouse button and moving left/right does the same.
        The left stick no longer tilts the device, and the right stick no
        longer drives the touch cursor.

    --retry-button=BUTTON
        (Private fork.) On the pause screen of a game with a retry button
        (Deflex), this button taps it. It does nothing anywhere else, e.g.
        --retry-button=X.

    --screenshot-every=SECONDS
        (Private fork.) Every SECONDS seconds, save the frame the app is
        presenting as touchHLE_screenshot_N.bmp in the touchHLE folder (for
        looking at a new app without being at the screen).

    --exit-button=BUTTON
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) In portrait, this button taps Exit on the
        pause screen and quits touchHLE from the main menu (the START! screen).
        It does nothing anywhere else, e.g. --exit-button=Back. The Esc key does
        the same. (The pad modules quit with Back and Esc too.)

    --record-scores
        (Private fork.) With OpenFeint faked (--fake-classes=OF), add each score
        the game submits to touchHLE_scores.txt in the touchHLE folder (time,
        app ID, leaderboard ID, score, player name, separated by tabs), for the
        launcher's high score tables.

    --player-name=NAME
        (Private fork.) The name --record-scores writes with each score (up to
        24 characters; default Player). The launcher gives it from its
        settings.

    --windowed
        (Private fork.) Undo --fullscreen: start in a window. The launcher's
        "Run windowed" setting gives it on the command line, after the options
        file.

    --plain-bars
        (Private fork.) Undo every style for the bars beside the picture
        (--bezel, --move-to-margin, --ambient-bars, --edge-bars,
        --edge-bars-texture, --starfield-bars): plain black. The launcher's
        "Display bezel: off".

    --two-player=N
        (Private fork.) Player N (1 or 2) of the launcher's two-player mode:
        only the pad in XInput slot N is used, and it keeps working while
        another window has the focus; the bars are plain black, the sound
        is at 70% and Gridrunner doesn't grab the mouse. Player 2 keeps its
        own saves (touchHLE_sandbox_p2) and log (touchHLE_log_p2.txt), so
        both players can run the same game. Give it on the command line.

    --window-rect=X,Y,WIDTH,HEIGHT
        (Private fork.) A borderless window at that place and size, in
        screen pixels, with the picture laid out in it as in fullscreen
        (e.g. one half of the screen in two-player mode). F11 does nothing.

    --no-enhancements
        (Private fork.) Undo --sharp-scaling, --nearest-present,
        --nearest-textures and --smooth-render-textures: the games' picture as
        it was. The launcher's "High detail: off".

    --smooth-render-textures
        (Private fork.) With --nearest-textures, leave textures the app draws
        into (render targets, often used for glows and trails) filtered the way
        the app asked, so only the game's artwork is made sharp. While the
        device is turned to landscape (e.g. Gridrunner's retro mode, which draws
        its whole screen into one) they are made sharp too.

    --scale-hack=...
        Set a scaling factor for the window. touchHLE will attempt to run the
        app with an increased internal resolution. This is a hack and there's
        no guarantee it will work correctly for all apps.

        The default is no scale hack, which is equivalent to a value of 1 (i.e.
        a scale of 1×).

        This is a natural number that is at least 1.

Device options:
    --device-family=...
        Specifies which device family should be emulated: iPhone or iPad.
        This only work if running app does support selected family
        (or the option will be ignored).

        If omitted, suitable device family would be deduced from application
        bundle itself, falling back to iPhone if needed.

        Accepted values are "iphone" and "ipad" respectively.

Game controller options:
    --deadzone=...
        Configures the size of the \"dead zone\" for analog stick inputs.

        The default value is 0.1, which means that 10% of the stick's range on
        the X and Y axes around the center position will be collapsed into a
        single point, so that movements in that range are ignored.

        This is a floating-point (decimal) number between 0 and 1.

    --disable-analog-stick-tilt-controls
        Disables mapping of analog stick input to simulated rotation of the
        device.

        By default, if touchHLE detects a game controller, its analog sticks
        will be used for simulated accelerometer tilt controls. When this
        happens, the real accelerometer (e.g. on an Android phone), if present,
        is ignored. By setting this option, the real accelerometer can be used
        even if a game controller is detected. This may be useful on a device
        that integrates both an accelerometer and a game controller, or if you
        do not want to disconnect your game controller.

        Note that touchHLE does not currently support accelerometers contained
        within game controllers.

    --x-tilt-range=...
    --y-tilt-range=...
        Set the simulated rotation range of the device axis mapped to the analog
        stick X or Y axis, or to the mouse cursor's X or Y position within the
        window (when the right mouse button is held).

        Positive X and Y are mapped to tilting the device rightward and forward
        respectively.

        By default, an analog stick's axis is mapped to a rotation range of 60°
        (30° in either direction). If you wanted a range of 90° on the X axis,
        you could use --x-tilt-range=90.

        For historical reasons, the device's X axis is mapped to the analog
        stick's Y axis and vice versa, as if the device is held in landscape.

        This is a floating-point (decimal) number of degrees, without a degree
        symbol. It may be negative.

    --x-tilt-offset=...
    --y-tilt-offset=...
        Offset the simulated angle of the device axis mapped to the analog stick
        X or Y axis, or to the mouse cursor's X or Y position within the window
        (when the right mouse button is held).

        Positive X and Y are mapped to tilting the device rightward and forward
        respectively.

        By default, the device is simulated as being level with the ground when
        the stick is in the center/neutral position. This option is intended for
        games that use a different angle relative to the ground as their neutral
        position. For example, if a game expects you to hold the device in a
        landscape orientation, with a 45° angle to the ground, you might use
        --y-tilt-offset=45.

        This is a floating-point (decimal) number of degrees, without a degree
        symbol. It may be negative.

    --button-to-touch=...
        Maps a button on a game controller to a point on the simulated touch
        screen of the device. Pressing the button will behave like touching that
        part of the screen.

        This is three parts separated by commas: the name of a button (DPadLeft,
        DPadUp, DPadRight, DPadDown, Start, LeftShoulder, Back, RightShoulder or Xbox-like
        A, B, X or Y), the X co-ordinate and the Y co-ordinate.
        The co-ordinates are floating-point (decimal) numbers. 0,0 is the
        top-left corner. The bottom-right corner is 320,480 if the app is in
        portrait, and 480,320 if the app is in landscape.

        For example, --button-to-touch=A,470,310 will make the A button simulate
        tapping in the bottom-right corner of the screen, for a landscape game.

        Except where specified in touchHLE_default_options.txt, this is not used
        by default. There is however a virtual cursor that is controlled by the
        right analog stick (tap/hold by pressing the stick or right shoulder
        button).

    --dpad-to-touch=...
        Maps a D-pad on a game controller to simulated finger movement within a
        region of the simulated touch screen of the device. This is useful for
        games where diagonals are used.

        This is four parts separated by commas: x,y,w,h. The co-ordinates
        are floating-point (decimal) numbers. 0,0 is the top-left corner. The
        bottom-right corner is 320,480 if the app is in portrait, and 480,320
        if the app is in landscape.

        For example, --dpad-to-touch=10,10,50,50 will simulate an 8-directional
        D-pad within a 50x50 pixel region starting at (10,10) on the screen.

        Note that this option takes precedence over any D-pad buttons defined
        with --button-to-touch.

        Except where specified in touchHLE_default_options.txt, this is not used
        by default. There is however a virtual cursor that is controlled by the
        right analog stick (tap/hold by pressing the stick or right shoulder
        button).

    --stick-to-touch=...
        Maps the left analog stick to simulated finger movement within a region
        of the simulated touch screen of the device. This is useful for games
        which use analog movement controls.

        This is four parts separated by commas: x,y,w,h. The co-ordinates
        are floating-point (decimal) numbers. 0,0 is the top-left corner. The
        bottom-right corner is 320,480 if the app is in portrait, and 480,320
        if the app is in landscape.

        For example, --stick-to-touch=10,10,100,100 will map the left analog
        stick to control a touch within a 100x100 pixel region starting at
        (10,10) on the screen.

        Except where specified in touchHLE_default_options.txt, this is not used
        by default. There is however a virtual cursor that is controlled by the
        right analog stick (tap/hold by pressing the stick or right shoulder
        button).

    --stick-to-swipe=...
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) Maps the left analog stick to a finger that
        sweeps continuously across the simulated touch screen, for games with
        relative "trackpad" style touch controls. The value is the finger's
        speed in points per second at full stick deflection, e.g.
        --stick-to-swipe=900. The finger touches down at the screen centre when
        the stick is pushed, lifts when it is released, and is recentred
        whenever it nears the edge of the screen.

    --mouse-to-swipe=...
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) Moving the mouse (with no button held) over
        the app slides the same finger as --stick-to-swipe, by the distance
        moved times this sensitivity, e.g. --mouse-to-swipe=1. The cursor is
        hidden while over the app. Left-click still taps at the cursor.

    --swipe-origin=...
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) Where the --stick-to-swipe/--mouse-to-swipe
        finger touches down, as x,y in screen points. Default: screen centre.

    --stick-to-icade
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) The left stick types the letters an iCade
        arcade cabinet sends for its joystick, for apps with iCade support.
        Pushing a direction past about a third of its travel holds it;
        diagonals hold two directions.

    --button-to-icade=...
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) While this controller button is held, the
        app sees an iCade arcade cabinet button (1-8, the cabinet's A-H) held,
        e.g. --button-to-icade=A,1. Can be given more than once.

    --icade-analog=...
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) Patches Gridrunner 1.02 or Super Ox Wars so
        that, with --stick-to-icade, the ship's speed follows how far the left
        stick is pushed instead of the iCade's on/off directions. The value is
        how much the ship may speed up per game tick, as a fraction of the
        game's full speed (their own iCade movement uses 0.08 in Gridrunner,
        0.15 in Super Ox Wars), optionally followed by a top
        speed (default 1, the game's own), e.g. --icade-analog=0.0625,1.25.
        Easing off, letting go and reversing take effect immediately.

    --button-to-rotate=...
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) Each press of this controller button turns
        the virtual device between portrait and landscape, as if it had been
        physically rotated, e.g. --button-to-rotate=Back.

    --rotate-three-ways
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) --button-to-rotate goes portrait,
        landscape left, landscape right, then back to portrait (Gridrunner:
        the iOS, C64 and VIC-20 versions).

    --upright-landscape-right
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) Show landscape right the way landscape
        left is shown, for apps that draw it upside down (Gridrunner's VIC-20
        version did on the iPad).

    --bezel=...
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) A picture (PNG or JPEG, path relative to the
        touchHLE folder) drawn across the whole screen behind the app, like an
        arcade cabinet's artwork, while fullscreen in portrait. It is scaled to
        cover the screen, so make it the screen's size or shape for best
        results, e.g. --bezel=gridrunner_bezel.png.

    --move-to-margin=X,Y,WIDTH,HEIGHT
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) Along with --bezel, cut this region out of
        the app's picture (portrait screen co-ordinates, top-left origin, 768x1024
        on an iPad) and show it in the margin to the right of the game instead.
        Can be given several times; the regions are stacked top to bottom in the
        order given. Only while upright in portrait. Gridrunner's scores are
        --move-to-margin=60,6,190,62 --move-to-margin=250,6,295,62
        --move-to-margin=600,6,168,62.

    --margin-scale=SCALE
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) How big the --move-to-margin regions are
        drawn, relative to the game picture. Default 1.5.

    --sharp-scaling
        (Private fork.) Fill the screen at any size while keeping pixels crisp
        and even: the picture is first enlarged by a whole number with no
        filtering, then smoothly stretched the rest of the way, so only pixel
        edges are blended. Use instead of --integer-scaling, e.g. for screens
        the game doesn't fit at a whole-number size.

    --landscape-fill-screen
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) With --integer-scaling and --fullscreen, stop
        using whole-number scaling while the device is in landscape, so the
        picture fills the screen's height instead.

    --landscape-button-to-touch=...
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) Like --button-to-touch, but used instead of
        it while the device is in landscape. Co-ordinates are in the landscape
        screen (1024x768 on an iPad), e.g.
        --landscape-button-to-touch=Start,512,285.

    --stick-mode-toggle=...
        (Private Gridrunner fork.) Pressing this button (any button name
        accepted by --button-to-touch, or Back) switches the --stick-to-swipe
        finger to a virtual stick and back. As a virtual stick, the finger is
        held at the swipe origin while the stick is pushed, displaced by up to
        the given radius in screen points, e.g. --stick-mode-toggle=Back,80.
        Combine with --button-to-touch on the same button to also tap the
        app's own control-type button.

    --stabilize-virtual-cursor=...
        Apply motion smoothing and a sticky radius to the virtual cursor
        (controlled by the right analog stick).

        Some apps are very sensitive to small finger movements and will always
        register an attempted "tap" as a scrolling gesture if the finger moves
        even very slightly. When combined with the low precision and shakiness
        of the virtual cursor, it can be difficult to use certain apps' menus.
        This option exists to compensate for this.

        The value is two floating-point (decimal) numbers separated by a comma.
        The first is the strength of the motion smoothing, and the second is a
        radius, in pixels, within which movement of the cursor will be ignored.
        To increase these values is to trade off responsiveness and precision
        respectively. Either number can be zero if desired. Note that the effect
        of the sticky radius is *not* shown on-screen!

        For example, --stabilize-virtual-cursor=0.1,10 will smooth out movements
        such that sharp movements take about half a second to complete, while
        movements within a 10px radius will be completely ignored.

Graphics driver options:
    --gles1=...
        Force touchHLE to use a particular OpenGL ES 1.1 implementation.
        This may be useful for troubleshooting graphics driver issues.

        --gles1=gles1_on_gl2 will use touchHLE's GLES1-on-GL2 layer.
        --gles1=gles1_native will use native OpenGL ES 1.1.

        When this option isn't in use, touchHLE will try each in order and use
        the first one that works.

Debugging options:
    --disable-direct-memory-access
        Force dynarmic to always access guest memory via the memory access
        callbacks, rather than using the fast direct access path (page tables).

    --gdb=...
        Starts touchHLE in debugging mode, listening for GDB remote serial
        protocol connections over TCP on the specified host and port.

        You can then connect to touchHLE with GDB and make use of its features
        to inspect memory and registers, set up software breakpoints, and
        continue or step execution.

        The host and port should be separated by a colon. The host can be a
        host name or an IP address. IPv6 addresses should be enclosed in square
        brackets, e.g. --gdb=[::1]:9001 for IPv6 loopback device port 9001.

    --no-error-popup
        Disables the graphical error message box (outputs to terminal/log file
        only).

    --dump=...
        Dumps some information about app/emulator to dump file. Check
        src/options.rs:parse_dump_options for the currently available options.

    --dump-file=...
        Pick the file for information to be dumped to, relative to the user
        data directory. Defaults to {user directory}/DUMP.txt. Note that this
        file will get truncated (deleted) if it already exists!

Other options:
    --preferred-languages=...
        Specifies a list of preferred languages to be reported to the app.

        This should be one or more ISO 639 language codes (usually two letters)
        in order of preference, separated by commas. For example,
        --preferred-languages=de,ja,en means you would prefer German, or failing
        that Japanese, or failing that English.

        If this option is not specified, the list comes from your operating
        system's language settings.

        Whether and how this preference is respected, and whether any particular
        language is supported, is determined entirely by the app.

    --headless
        Run in headless mode. touchHLE will not create a window, so there will
        be no graphical output and no input. Only useful for command-line apps.
        Implies --no-error-popup.

    --args
        All arguments following this option will be passed to the app (via C's
        argc/argv) rather than processed by touchHLE. This option can't be used
        via touchHLE_options.txt or touchHLE_default_options.txt.

    --print-fps
        Logs the current framerate (FPS) to the console once per second.

    --fps-limit=...
        Modify or disable the framerate (FPS) limit.

        By default, touchHLE limits the framerate of an app to 60fps, because
        this is required for accurate emulation. Some apps can however run
        correctly at a higher framerate than normal.

        This is either 'off', to disable the framerate limit, or otherwise
        a floating-point (decimal) number of frames per second.

        Note that many apps have an internal timer that determines how often
        they present frames; increasing the limit will not increase their
        framerate, but may make it less consistent.

    --force-composition
        Forces presentation of renderbuffer as if no fullscreen EAGL layer is
        present, i.e. renderbuffer will go through the compositor.

        Enabling this option may solve some rendering issues, but implies
        a performance hit which is undesirable in most of the cases.

    --allow-network-access
        Allow apps running in touchHLE to access the Internet and the local network.

    --ignore-gl-errors
        Ignore host OpenGL errors as queried by glGetError function.
        This only affects return value of glGetError as seen by the guest
        and nothing else. Should be enabled only for apps which really
        need this behaviour.

    --zero-stack-after-guest-to-host-call=<bytes>
        After a guest-to-host call returns, zero out the given number of
        bytes immediately below the current stack pointer (i.e. the unused
        scratch region of the guest's full-descending stack).
        This option exists because a guest-to-host call leaves that region
        untouched, whereas a real call on iOS could leave it in a "dirty"
        state. This can make a difference for apps that store data on the
        stack and branch based on the stack's content. There is normally
        no reason to enable this option. The number of bytes must be
        chosen carefully on a per-app basis.
